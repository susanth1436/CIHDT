6.5  SAMPLE WORKFLOW

The run reproduced below is a complete twelve-step walkthrough on a mixed
eight-device fleet with seed 1337, executed end to end in 14.9 seconds on a
laptop-class machine with no GPU. It is reproducible with the command

    python cli.py --walk

on the library versions pinned in requirements.txt. Every figure quoted in
this section was produced by that run; none has been rounded for
presentation or carried over from a different execution. The same sequence
is available through the web console, where each step is a separate page and
each action is a form submission, and through the JSON API, where each step
is a single endpoint.

Table 6.1 lists the twelve operator actions and what the system returned for
each. The sections that follow expand the five moments where the framework's
contribution is visible.

6.5.1  Steps 1 to 4 — Establishing a baseline worth trusting

The fleet is assembled one device at a time rather than generated in bulk,
because everything downstream is shaped by that choice: the labelled corpus
in Step 6 is built from the profiles of the devices actually deployed, so
the detector is trained on the network it will police. Six distinct device
types were chosen deliberately, since their baselines differ by three orders
of magnitude and a heterogeneous fleet is a harder detection problem than a
uniform one.

Collection and learning are kept apart. Step 2 buffers 45 ticks — 45 ticks
multiplied by 8 devices gives 360 observations — and those observations stay
at the gateway until Step 3 folds them in. In an automatic system telemetry
flows straight into the model of normal, which is precisely the door a slow
poisoning attack walks through.

Step 4 fits a ridge-regularised vector autoregression per twin over three
lags. For smart_camera_01 the fitted model expected CPU usage to fall from
32.88 to 25.45 and outbound traffic to rise from 2,356.97 to 2,765.47 kbps.
The point forecast, however, is not the valuable output of this step: each
fit also measures its own residual spread, which is what gives every device
an individual tolerance. A rock-steady temperature sensor receives a tight
envelope and a bursty smart speaker a wide one, and that is what prevents
the consistency score in Step 5 from degenerating into a single threshold
that is either deaf on noisy devices or hysterical on quiet ones.


6.5.2  Step 5 — Committing before observing

Each armed twin commits twenty predicted trajectories over a five-tick
horizon, and only then is a live tick taken and scored against them. The
ordering is the security invariant of the whole framework: the futures must
exist before the observation they are used to judge, or the comparison means
nothing.

On this healthy fleet consistency ranged from 0.467 on smart_bulb_02 to
0.892 on smart_camera_02, against a flag threshold of 0.35. No device was
flagged, which is the result the step is meant to produce. Two devices
showed strain in a named plane — topology on smart_bulb_02, network on
temperature_sensor_01 — without crossing the threshold, and that naming is
itself diagnostic: the plane that moves indicates what kind of attack is
occurring, not merely that one has.

A clean baseline here is a precondition for Step 12. The quantity the
self-healing monitor watches is disagreement between the twin and the
classifier, and a twin that cries wolf on healthy traffic makes that signal
worthless.


6.5.3  Step 6 — Two independent detectors

A 2,660-row labelled corpus was generated from the deployed fleet's own
profiles and split 1,649 for training and 1,011 for held-out evaluation.
Model v1 trained in 0.96 seconds and reached 0.9565 accuracy across seven
classes, with a false-positive rate of 0.0488 and a false-negative rate of
0.0307. Its weakest class was data exfiltration at 87% recall, and the
feature it leaned on most was log_port_diversity.

Accuracy is the least interesting number on that list. A backdoored model
scores well on accuracy — producing exactly that outcome is the purpose of a
backdoor — so the two error rates, and the poison resistance measured in
Step 9, are what indicate whether the model can be trusted.

A DDoS attack was then launched by hand against smart_camera_01 and a brute
force attack against smart_camera_02, and six ticks were run through the
full gateway path. Of 48 observations, 12 came from devices genuinely under
attack; the model caught all 12 and the twin independently flagged the same
12, with no false alarms on the six healthy devices. Twin consistency on the
attacked devices collapsed to between 0.000 and 0.006, against roughly 0.5
to 0.9 for healthy traffic.

That the two detectors agree is what makes Step 12 possible. They judge the
same events by independent means — the twin against its own committed
forecast, the model against what it learned from the corpus — so when a
poisoned model later falls silent while the twin keeps flagging, that
divergence is the only unlabelled evidence of compromise available.


6.5.4  Step 8 — The decision the framework exists to make

The accept gate is calibrated before any hostile update is judged. Eight
honest contributions were generated from held-out telemetry and scored
against an audit bank of 187 committed scenarios. They produced a mean
Counterfactual Consistency Score of 0.9168 with a spread of 0.0068, and the
threshold was set three spreads below the honest mean at 0.8568. The
threshold is therefore derived from this fleet rather than hard-coded, which
is the difference between a defence that transfers to a new network and one
that works only on the machine it was written on.

Thirteen contributions were then submitted — one honest, twelve hostile
across the five poisoning strategies — and all thirteen decisions were
correct.

Two rows warrant comment. From the second sybil shard onward the gate rises
from 0.857 to 0.907, because the burst of previously unseen contributor
identities triggers the novelty penalty; the first shard is still judged at
the calibrated threshold, since at that point nothing unusual has yet been
observed. The clean-label row is the result that separates this framework
from distributional defences: its label mix barely moves, so every check
based on label distribution admits it, and it is caught only by the
cross-check of its labels against the trusted model's own reading of its
rows.

The label-flipping decision is worth following in full, because it shows
what the console reports rather than merely what it concludes. The update
was grafted onto the trusted core, a shadow model was trained, and that
shadow was put in front of all 187 bank scenarios in 316 milliseconds. It
scored 0.629 against a gate of 0.857, missing by 0.228. The component that
drove the decision was label agreement at 0.298 against a weight of 0.20 —
that is, the degree to which the update's labels match the trusted model's
own reading of the same rows. Accepting it would have left only 81% of data
exfiltration scenarios still detectable. The contributor's reputation stood
at 0.889 against a floor of 0.70, so reputation alone would not have stopped
it. Attribution named label flipping, and the update genuinely was label
flipping.

Nothing was applied at that point. The verdict is a recommendation until the
operator acts on it, and it can be overruled in either direction, with the
override written to the audit trail either way — because a security control
a human cannot inspect and countermand is not a control but an oracle.


6.5.5  Steps 9 to 12 — Immunize, retrain, and recover

Step 9 synthesised 126 vaccine rows from the committed futures, split evenly
across three families: 42 boundary rows, 42 relabel rows and 42 trigger
rows. Each of the five poisoning strategies was then rehearsed — crafted,
grafted onto the current pool at a realistic federated share, used to
retrain a probe, and measured by how many of the 106 attack scenarios
survived. Overall poison resistance was 0.9736 against a gate of 0.78,
weakest against backdoor and clean-label poisoning at 0.9528 each and
strongest against gradual poisoning at 1.0000.

Step 10 held twelve updates, each retained intact with its attributed
strategy and the evidence behind the attribution. For the clean-label
update, that evidence was the cross-plane physics: a power-per-CPU agreement
of 0.71, events-per-command of 0.84 and outbound-over-inbound of 0.87,
indicating features altered without keeping the planes mutually consistent.

Step 11 trained a candidate on 2,015 rows, including the 126 vaccine rows,
in 2.4 seconds. It scored 0.9674 on the held-out evaluation set against the
incumbent's 0.9565, with poison resistance 0.9925, a false-positive rate of
0.0305 and a pool of 1,889 rows. All four promotion gates passed and the
candidate was promoted as model v2. Rejection at this point would have been
a successful outcome rather than an error: the gates exist so that a
contaminated pool cannot install a model worse than the one already running,
and the poison-resistance gate in particular is the one an accuracy-only
check would miss.

Step 12 evaluated model health over the last 48 observations. The twin
flagged 12, the model independently flagged 12, and they agreed on all 12,
giving a health score of 1.000 against a floor of 0.4 and no degradation
signal. A rollback was then forced, in order to exercise the recovery path
on a healthy system: model v2 was marked untrusted, v1 was reactivated as
the newest version still trusted, rows were purged by contributor rather
than wholesale, and the health baseline was reset, because a different model
has a different notion of normal. Healing is rollback, purge and retrain in
that order and never in place — poison cannot be subtracted from a trained
model, only returned from, which is also why the registry never overwrites a
version and why every pooled row records who contributed it.


6.5.6  What this run establishes, and what it does not

The run demonstrates that the twelve stages compose: that futures committed
in advance can be scored against reality, that the resulting score separates
honest contributions from five distinct poisoning strategies without a
single error across thirteen decisions, that the accept threshold can be
derived at run time from the deployed fleet, and that promotion and recovery
are gated rather than automatic.

It does not establish superiority over reactive baselines, because no
baseline was re-implemented and run against the same fleet; that comparison
is Objective O7 and is delivered in Phase II. It is a single seed on a
single fleet, so the figures quoted characterise this run rather than a
distribution over runs. The attack strategies are the five rehearsed in the
taxonomy, and an adaptive adversary with knowledge of the Counterfactual
Consistency Score itself is outside the scope of this evaluation.
